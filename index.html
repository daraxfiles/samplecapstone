About the learning program

Three ways to learn. All are free, all are confidential, and you can switch at any time.

How our classes work

Every learner gets a short, relaxed conversation with a coordinator first. There is no test to pass. We talk about what you want to read or write, like a work form, a driver's manual, or a book for your grandkids, and then we suggest the program that fits.

Home / Get Started

Your first class, step by step

Here's exactly what happens, so there are no surprises. Most people are reading with a tutor within two weeks.

  1. Reach out

    Call us, text us, or fill out the short contact form. You can also ask a friend or family member to contact us for you.

  2. Have a welcome chat

    A coordinator meets with you for about 30 minutes, in person or by phone. You'll talk about your goals and your schedule. This is a conversation, not a test.

  3. Get matched

    Within one week, we match you with a tutor or a group based on your goals, your schedule, and the library that's easiest for you to reach.

  4. Go to your first session

    Your tutor will meet you at the library's front desk. Your first session is mostly getting to know each other and picking something you want to read.

  5. Keep going at your pace

    Every few months, you and your tutor look back at your progress and set a new goal. There's no deadline and no final exam.

What to bring

  • Nothing is required
  • Glasses, if you use them
  • Anything you want help reading, like mail or a work form

You do not need: an ID, proof of address, or immigration papers.

Learning resources

Practice between classes with these short, simple strategies. Try one at a time.

Four ways to figure out a new word

When you get stuck on a word, try these in order. Most of the time, one of them will work.

  1. Look for a part you know. In "careful," you may already know "care."
  2. Say the first sound, then guess. Use the sentence to help you guess a word that makes sense.
  3. Read to the end of the sentence. The words after the hard word often give you a clue.
  4. Break it into chunks. "Appointment" becomes ap-point-ment.
Tip: It's fine to skip a word and come back. Good readers do it all the time.

Reading with children

You don't have to read every word to share a book. Talking about the pictures and asking "What do you think happens next?" builds reading skills for both of you.

Free children's books are available at every Riverbend library. Ask us which library has the family reading corner.

Home / FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Select a question to see the answer.

Is it really free?

Yes. Classes, books, and materials are always free. We're funded by donations and local grants.

Will anyone find out I'm taking classes?

No. Everything you tell us is private. We never share your name or information with employers, landlords, or government agencies.

I'm learning English. Is this for me?

Our classes are for adults who speak English but want to read and write it better. If you're new to English, we'll connect you with a free English class at a partner organization.

What if I miss a class?

That's okay. Just let your tutor know when you can. Life happens, and your spot is safe.

Can someone come with me to the first visit?

Of course. Many learners bring a friend or family member for support.

Still have a question? Contact our learner coordinator.
